## Page anchors in use

- Study options: «خلّينا نلاقي خيارات بتناسبك.» / «ابدأ باهتماماتك، معدّلك، وميزانيتك.»
- Experience: «مش لازم تكون أول وظيفة عشان تكون خبرة.» plus «مشروع جامعة، تطوّع، أو شغل جزئي: احكي شو كان دورك.»
- Roles: «شوف شو بتطلب الوظيفة، وحدّد خطوتك الجايّة.»
- Funding: «تكاليف الدراسة شاغلة بالك؟» followed by standard Arabic for conditions.
- My plan: «كل خطواتك بمكان واحد.» / «ارجع لخياراتك المحفوظة وكمّل من وين وقفت.»
- Microcopy: «لسّه ما أضفت تجربة مرتبطة بهاي المهارة.», «لسّه بتستكشف؟ عادي، ابدأ باللي بهمّك.», «انحفظت بخطتك.» (shown only after the save is confirmed in browser storage).

## How the voice works

- **Who is speaking:** a helpful older student who knows the system. Short sentences, direct actions, no promises.
- **Arabic register:** light Jordanian spoken Arabic for headlines, encouragement and buttons (شو، بدك، الجايّة، خلّينا، يلّا). Standard Arabic stays wherever precision matters: admission minimums, funding conditions, repayment, privacy, evidence states, review rules and employer pages.
- **English:** written for English-speaking students in Jordan, with the same personality, not translated word for word.
- **Local situations, not symbols:** examples are a Tawjihi student comparing programs, a student-club career day, part-time work and costs in JOD. No flags or decorative clichés. Fictional people (Lina, demo employers) stay labelled.
- **No assumptions:** copy never assumes strong grades, money, connections or a fixed career plan («لسّه بتستكشف؟ عادي»), and never promises admission, funding or a job.

## Needs review by students in Jordan

1. Gender: Arabic second-person verbs and some adjectives (محتار، جاهز، بتستكشف، إنت) are masculine by default. Inclusive alternatives were used where natural (خطتي، خياراتك، «بدون تسجيل»), but the headline and card titles still read masculine. Ask students whether forms like «محتار/ة» or rephrasing feel more natural.
2. «الاستعداد للشغل» as a navigation label: clear, or would «التحضير للشغل» / «الشغل» read better?
3. «شو بدك تدرس؟» as a card title next to a less colloquial description: does the mix feel natural?
4. Spelling of colloquial words in Arabic script (الجايّة with shadda, بالزبط، هون، هاي).
5. English «What’s your next move?» versus the slogan «Your next step, clearer.»: do the two read as one voice?
6. Employer-facing pages are deliberately kept in standard Arabic; confirm this suits Jordanian employers.

No audience validation has taken place.
